# Chapter 6 — Tailoring for Resonance

## Core idea
Tailoring is the work of connecting a sound business insight to the priorities of the specific organization and the people involved in its decision. It is not cosmetic personalization or changing vocabulary to match a personality; it helps each stakeholder see a relevant business outcome.

## Map from broad context to the individual
1. **Industry:** market forces, regulation, competition, and operating conditions.
2. **Company:** strategy, recent events, business goals, and constraints.
3. **Role:** responsibilities, metrics, and risks owned by the stakeholder.
4. **Individual:** personal priorities, concerns, incentives, and decision criteria.

Use what can be researched centrally for industry and company context, then validate role-level and personal assumptions in conversation. Authors describe **functional-bias cards** as a way to capture a role’s key outcomes, metrics, recurring questions, and concerns. They are preparation aids, not psychological profiles or substitutes for listening.

## Build consensus rather than bypass it
The book reports that decision makers prioritize broad support across their organization and ease of working with a supplier, while influencers and end users value professionalism, trust, and useful ideas. Authors’ SEC research attributes 53% of measured B2B loyalty to the sales experience; treat that as a study-specific result, not a current universal ratio. The practical implication is not to bypass the buying group to reach the most senior person. Help stakeholders understand and communicate the value in terms relevant to their responsibilities.

A **value plan** can document the shared project objective, stakeholders, the outcome each person needs, their principal concern, and evidence or an action that addresses it. Discuss the plan with the customer; do not convert it into a box-checking exercise or pressure for signatures.

## Example: Solae
The case illustrates how a technical offering required engagement with additional roles in marketing, manufacturing, and procurement. A functional mapping tool linked each stakeholder’s outcomes to supplier capabilities; a project plan recorded the team’s agreement and remaining concerns. The lesson is that tailoring can be systematized while still being validated in the account.

## Limits
A role template is a hypothesis, not proof of an individual’s priorities. Re-check it with the person. The book’s customer loyalty findings are from its own research period; they do not establish current market behavior. Broad stakeholder support is useful when it reflects real agreement, not merely collected approvals.
